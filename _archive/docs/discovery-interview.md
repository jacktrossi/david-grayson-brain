# GraysonOS Discovery Interview

### A conversation guide for Jack → David Grayson

---

> **Purpose.** GraysonOS will be configured around how David *actually* works — not how we assume he works. This interview is the foundation. Over the next 30–45 minutes, the goal is to map David's real daily workflow: where his time goes, what he dreads, what slips through the cracks, and what would genuinely change his day if it happened automatically.
>
> **How to run it.** This is a conversation, not a form. Ask the question, then stop talking. Let David fill the silence. Use the bulleted probes only when an answer stays on the surface — they're there to dig, not to march through. Take notes in the margins. The richest material usually comes from the follow-ups and the tangents, not the first answer.
>
> **At the end**, fill out the Configuration Notes Template (last page). That sheet is what we build from.

---

## 1. The Daily Reality

> *Why this matters: before we touch any specific tool, we need the shape of David's day — the rhythm, the bookends, the moments he feels in control versus underwater. This tells us when to surface a brief, when to stay quiet, and what "working well" even means for him.*

**1.1** Walk me through a typical morning — from the moment you wake up to your first meeting.
- What's the very first thing you reach for? Phone, coffee, laptop?
- Is there a routine, or does it depend on the day?
- Where are you when you start working — home, office, in transit?

**1.2** What's the first thing you check when you sit down to work?
- Email? Calendar? Markets? Something else?
- Are you checking it because you want to, or because you're afraid of missing something?

**1.3** When in the day do you feel most on top of things — and when do you feel most overwhelmed?
- What's happening in those overwhelmed moments? Is it volume, surprises, or context-switching?
- Is it the same time every day, or event-driven?

**1.4** What time of day do you do your best focused work?
- Is that time protected, or does it get eaten by other things?

**1.5** What does "a good day" look like — versus a day that goes sideways?
- What's the difference between the two? One missed thing, or a pile-up?
- When a day goes sideways, what usually started it?

> **What to listen for:** the natural timing for the morning brief (and whether it should land before or after he opens email); which channel he checks first thing (that's where GraysonOS should put the highest-priority signal); and his protected focus window, which we must learn *not* to interrupt.

## 2. Email

> *Why this matters: email is the single biggest time sink for most advisors. With eight accounts, David's surface area is huge. We need volume, the worst offender, the act/read/ignore split, and — most important — what he misses. The gap between "what arrives" and "what he acts on" is where GraysonOS earns its keep.*

**2.1** Roughly how many emails a day do you get across all your accounts?
- Is that spread evenly, or does one account dominate?
- Has the volume been creeping up, or is it steady?

**2.2** Which account is the biggest problem?
- Is it the volume there, or the *importance* of what lands there?
- Is that the one you check most, or the one you avoid?

**2.3** What kinds of emails do you actually need to act on, versus just read, versus ignore entirely?
- Can you give me a recent example of each?
- What makes something "act on this now" for you?

**2.4** What falls through the cracks most often — what do you miss, or respond to too late?
- When that happens, what's the cost? An awkward apology, a lost opportunity, a compliance issue?
- Is it the same *type* of thing that slips each time?

**2.5** Do you have a system for flagging things that need follow-up? Does it work?
- Stars, folders, leaving it unread, a notepad?
- Where does that system break down?

**2.6** How long does it take you to get through email in the morning?
- And does "getting through it" mean handled, or just *seen*?

**2.7** Are there specific senders you always need to respond to same-day?
- Who, specifically? Clients, attorney, accountant, family?
- If something from them slipped a day, would that be a real problem?

> **What to listen for:** the priority ordering of the eight accounts (does his lived reality match the CLAUDE.md order: client-facing → legal → investments → finance-pro → admin → domains → travel → personal?); the exact senders that should trigger same-day alerts; and the recurring *category* of dropped email — that becomes the first triage rule we harden.

## 3. Clients

> *Why this matters: client relationships are the business. We need to know how David tracks what's open with each client, how he triages inbound, and which follow-ups slip. Anything manual here is a candidate for automation — and anything dropping is a risk to the firm.*

**3.1** How many active clients do you have right now?
- Are they all roughly equal, or are there a handful that take most of your attention?
- Any newer relationships that need extra care right now?

**3.2** How do you keep track of what's open or pending with each client?
- Is it in your head, in email, in a CRM, on paper?
- Could you tell me right now what's outstanding with your top three clients? How long would that take you?

**3.3** When a client emails you, what's your process — respond right away, batch it, or something else?
- Does that change based on which client it is?
- Do you ever sit on a client email longer than you'd like?

**3.4** How do you prep for a client meeting? What do you wish you had in front of you that you don't?
- Walk me through prepping for the *next* one on your calendar.
- Is there information you end up hunting for every single time?

**3.5** Do you have a way of knowing which client relationships need attention versus which are healthy?
- How would you know if you'd gone quiet on someone for too long?
- Has a relationship ever cooled because something slipped?

**3.6** What's the follow-up that most often slips — action items from calls, quarterly check-ins, something else?
- Where in the chain does it break — capturing it, remembering it, or doing it?

> **What to listen for:** which clients to load first (Smith Capital, Harris Group, Mercer are in CLAUDE.md — confirm and expand); whether a meeting prep brief is the highest-value early automation; and the specific follow-up that slips, which maps directly to the `action_items` workflow.

## 4. Calendar & Scheduling

> *Why this matters: this tells us how David manages time and whether he walks into meetings prepared or scrambling. Scheduling friction and unprotected focus time are both fixable — but only if we know which one actually hurts.*

**4.1** How far ahead is your calendar typically planned?
- Days, weeks, a rolling mess?
- Do you trust your calendar, or do things live outside it?

**4.2** Do you feel like you go into meetings prepared, or do you find yourself scrambling?
- When you scramble, what are you scrambling *for*?
- How much lead time would you actually want before a meeting to feel ready?

**4.3** What's the most common scheduling headache you deal with?
- Double-bookings, time zones, back-and-forth, no-shows?

**4.4** Do you block time for focused work, or does the calendar just fill up?
- If you block it, does the block survive, or does it get overwritten?

**4.5** How do you handle rescheduling — is that a back-and-forth that wastes time?
- Who initiates most reschedules — you or the other side?
- How many emails does a single reschedule usually take?

> **What to listen for:** the right lead time for prep briefs (4.2); whether calendar routing across the eight calendars matches reality; and whether reschedule back-and-forth is painful enough to warrant automated handling.

## 5. Repetitive Tasks

> *Why this matters: this is the gold mine. Every "I do this over and over" is a direct automation target. Push hard here — the things people repeat are so habitual they often don't notice them. Make him slow down and actually count.*

**5.1** What do you do every single day that you wish just happened automatically?
- If you could delete one recurring chore from your day, which one?

**5.2** What do you find yourself writing over and over — similar emails, similar updates?
- Could you describe the last one? What's the template hiding inside it?
- How many versions of "the same email" do you send in a week?

**5.3** What do you copy-paste or re-type regularly?
- Disclaimers, account details, scheduling links, intros?
- Where do you copy it *from* each time?

**5.4** If an assistant watched everything you did for a week, what would they say you waste the most time on?
- What would surprise *them* — and what would surprise *you*?

**5.5** What's the task you dread most each week?
- What makes it dreadful — the time, the tedium, or the stakes?
- When do you usually end up doing it? (Friday afternoon, Sunday night?)

> **What to listen for:** concrete, nameable repeated actions — these become the first three automations. Pay special attention to repeated *writing* (email templating + the mandatory client disclaimer) and repeated *lookups* (data we can pre-fetch into a brief).

## 6. Information & Research

> *Why this matters: David is an investment advisor — market awareness is part of the job, but it's also a time sink and a re-synthesis trap. We want to know his sources and what a perfect morning brief contains, so the brief replaces the hunting rather than adding to it.*

**6.1** How do you stay current on markets and investments — what sources do you check?
- Which ones do you genuinely read versus open out of habit?
- Where do you go *first* when something big moves?

**6.2** How much time do you spend on research versus client work on a typical day?
- Does that split feel right, or is one crowding out the other?

**6.3** Do you find yourself re-reading or re-synthesizing the same kind of information repeatedly?
- Is it the same tickers, sectors, or themes each time?
- Are you rebuilding the same summary for different clients?

**6.4** What would a perfect morning market brief look like for you?
- How long — a glance, or a deep read?
- What's *in* it? Indices, holdings, client-relevant news, calendar of earnings?
- What's the one thing that, if it were in there, you'd open it every single morning?

> **What to listen for:** the exact contents and length of his ideal market brief (6.4) — this defines the daily research automation; and any sign he's manually rebuilding the same summaries, which we can template.

## 7. Travel

> *Why this matters: travel is high-friction and detail-heavy — loyalty numbers, seat prefs, itineraries spread across confirmations. We have his preferences on file (Delta/SkyMiles, Marriott/Bonvoy, National/Emerald, aisle, TSA PreCheck); this is about frequency and where it breaks.*

**7.1** How often are you traveling for work?
- Steady, or seasonal/event-driven?
- Mostly day trips, or multi-night?

**7.2** What's the most frustrating part of booking and managing travel?
- The booking itself, the itinerary juggling, or the day-of logistics?

**7.3** Do you have someone who books for you, or do you do it yourself?
- If yourself — what step do you put off the longest?

**7.4** Have you ever missed a loyalty benefit, or had a booking go wrong because something wasn't organized?
- What happened, and what would have caught it?

> **What to listen for:** travel frequency (decides how much to invest here early); whether itinerary parsing into the `trips` table and Travel calendar is worth front-loading; and confirmation that the stored loyalty/seat preferences are still current.

## 8. Domains & Tech

> *Why this matters: quick validation section. We mostly need to confirm the inventory and catch anything expiring soon — this maps straight to the `domains` table and the 30-day expiry alert rule.*

**8.1** Roughly how many domains do you own, and do you know when they expire?
- Are these all business, or a mix of business and personal/speculative?

**8.2** Are they all on auto-renew, or do you manage that manually?
- Has one ever lapsed by accident?

**8.3** Any domains you're not sure what to do with?
- Anything you'd sell, or quietly let go?

> **What to listen for:** any domain expiring inside 30 days (immediate HIGH-priority action item); auto-renew gaps to flag; and whether the January domain audit is something he'd actually want surfaced.

## 9. The Ideal State

> *Why this matters: end on the vision. After 40 minutes of pain points, this lifts the conversation to what "great" feels like — and the answer to "the one thing" usually names the first automation more clearly than any earlier question did.*

**9.1** If this system worked exactly the way you wanted, what would your morning look like?
- Walk me through it, hour by hour.
- What would you *stop* doing entirely?

**9.2** What's the one thing — if it were handled automatically — that would make the biggest difference in your day?
- Why that one? What does it cost you today?

**9.3** What would "I never have to think about that again" feel like for you?
- What's currently taking up mental space that shouldn't?

**9.4** Is there anything else that's been on your mind that we haven't covered?
- Anything you assumed I'd ask about and I didn't?
- Anything you're nervous about handing off to a system?

> **What to listen for:** 9.2 is the headline — it names the first automation. 9.4 often surfaces the thing David *won't* let go of (which goes in "do not automate"). Note the emotional weight, not just the words.
